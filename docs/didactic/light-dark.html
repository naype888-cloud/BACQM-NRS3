Light share and dark share — what each axis resolves

What a pair of observables resolves is its light share: the squared cosine between the two fluctuation vectors. What it cannot resolve is the dark share, the Robertson defect over the product of the uncertainties. They add up to one on every axis. A closed axis of three positions at its maximal current state resolves 4/5 and keeps 1/5 dark; as the ring grows both tend to 1/2.

At the maximal current state, the c of the closed axis, three positions resolve 4/5 and keep 1/5 dark; the open axis with three positions keeps 0. For larger rings the share is not monotone, it goes to 1/2 from both sides (RingSzego). What the window needs is not a value but c > 1: at the ring limit c = √2 it is 0.1444, positive because the infinite limit does not saturate the Gaussian (angle 45°, not 0). The first two plots are computed live from the matrices of the ring; Ωb is the measured datum of each instrument and the spread between fronts is the error of each instrument.
