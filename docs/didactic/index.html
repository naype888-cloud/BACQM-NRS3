Uncertainty on the three-torus, in eight stops

Three closed axes, x, y and z, and on each one a position and a transport that do not commute. Each stop asks one question, lets you play with it in the browser, and ends with the Lean theorem that answers it.

  1. 1

    Why can't position and transport both be sharp on a closed axis?

    Every state obeys det Σ ≥ |det Ω|; at the maximal current state of T³, det Σ = (125/64)·|det Ω|.
    Every torus has its star →Lean
  2. 2

    How much of each axis can be resolved?

    Light + dark = 1 on every axis; at the maximal current state each axis resolves 4/5 and keeps 1/5 dark.
    Light and dark on T³ →Lean
  3. 3

    Where does a region keep its information?

    On its boundary: a cut of T³ has two walls, the plane and the seam.
    Holography: open cube and three-torus →Lean
  4. 4

    How many bits fit on the boundary, and what does full look like?

    S = 2·dy·dz·log 2, whatever the depth; it is reached only by the uniform distribution.

    What does a full region look like up close, and is the torus curved?

    Each step is open half of the time and fluctuates 1/4; T³ is flat, the 16-cell has K = 4.

  6. 6

    Why does every axis stay inside a light cone?

    The axis vector of every state lies in the closed future cone, and SL(2, ℂ) never moves it out.
    SL(2, ℂ) on T³ →Lean

    Why does the Dirac operator on T³ see eight corners?

    D² = T_x² + T_y² + T_z² + m²; on even sizes the eight corners of the momentum cube are its doublers.
    The hopping and the eight doublers →Lean
  8. 8

    How does a cut give the Einstein equation?

    Every cut carries η = 1/(4ℓ_P²); with the Clausius relation on every light sheet, R_kk = 8πℓ_P² T_kk.
