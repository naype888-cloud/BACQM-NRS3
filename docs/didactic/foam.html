The dark foam — and why the torus is flat

A region filled to the top is not quiet. Look at one of its walls: every step is open half of the time and flips as much as a bit can, yet the average over time is perfectly flat. Curvature, read the way Bochner did, tests a landscape on the space: on T³ it never bends, the torus is flat, while the 16-cell of the fibred box has curvature 4.

The torus is flat

Curvature, read the way Bochner did: put a landscape on the space and look, at every point, at how its steepness changes around that point. On a sphere the reading is always at least some positive amount K times the steepness; on a saddle it goes negative somewhere. On T³ Lean proves the reading is never negative, for any landscape, and a straight ramp gives exactly zero: no K > 0 works. The torus is flat. The closed space with a fourth, fibred axis (the 16-cell) reads K = 4 instead.

What is still open: the conjecture that curvature appears where the field moves away from the even, full state. It is not in Lean yet. Back to bits on the boundary · holography.

What is exact here

  • Lean Under the full (uniform) distribution every step is open with probability 1/2 and fluctuates 1/4, the most a bit can.
  • Lean The mean field is half the field of the fully open wall: flat on average.
  • Lean The Bochner form of T³ is a sum of squares: curvature ≥ 0, and no positive bound — T³ is flat.
  • Lean The 16-cell has curvature 4; the path has CD(0, 2).
  • Open That curvature appears where the field moves away from the even, full state.
